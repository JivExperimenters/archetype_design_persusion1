## What it is
De Stijl was a Dutch art and design movement that began in 1917 around the journal *De Stijl*, founded by Theo van Doesburg. The movement included artists and designers such as Piet Mondrian and Gerrit Rietveld. De Stijl aimed to create a universal visual language using simplified forms, straight lines, and a limited color palette.

De Stijl is closely connected to Modernism because it rejected unnecessary decoration and focused on abstraction, geometric structure, and functional visual organization. Its ideas influenced painting, architecture, furniture, typography, and graphic design.

## Recognize and apply it

<div style="display:grid; grid-template-columns:repeat(3, minmax(0, 1fr)); gap:14px; margin:22px 0;">
  <div style="background:#ff3b30; border:4px solid #111111; padding:16px; color:#ffffff;">
    <h3 style="margin:0 0 10px; font-size:1.2rem;">Geometric forms</h3>
    <p style="margin:0; line-height:1.5;">Designs use straight lines, blocks, and clean rectangles to create rational structure.</p>
  </div>
  <div style="background:#f5d000; border:4px solid #111111; padding:16px; color:#111111;">
    <h3 style="margin:0 0 10px; font-size:1.2rem;">Primary colors</h3>
    <p style="margin:0; line-height:1.5;">Red, yellow, and blue are paired with black, white, and gray for maximum clarity.</p>
  </div>
  <div style="background:#1d4ed8; border:4px solid #111111; padding:16px; color:#ffffff;">
    <h3 style="margin:0 0 10px; font-size:1.2rem;">Asymmetrical balance</h3>
    <p style="margin:0; line-height:1.5;">Weight is distributed through offset blocks and lines instead of strict symmetry.</p>
  </div>
</div>

These features are useful when a design needs to feel structured, clean, and intentionally modern. For a hero section, I could use a grid of rectangles, a limited palette of red, yellow, blue, and neutral tones, and a clear placement for the headline and call to action.

  <div style="background:#f6f1ea; border:4px solid #111111; padding:18px; box-shadow:8px 8px 0 #ff3b30;">
    <h3 style="margin-top:0; margin-bottom:12px; font-size:1.5rem;">Example 1</h3>
    <p><strong>Creator, title, date, institution:</strong> Gerrit Rietveld, <em>Red and Blue Chair</em>, 1918–1923, The Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York.</p>
    <p><strong>Direct collection/source link:</strong> <a href="https://www.moma.org/collection/works/4044">MoMA collection record</a></p>
    <p><strong>Exact image:</strong> <a href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/09/Gerrit_thomas_rietveld%2C_red-blue_chair%2C_1946-56_ca.jpg">Direct image file</a></p>
    <img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/09/Gerrit_thomas_rietveld%2C_red-blue_chair%2C_1946-56_ca.jpg" alt="Gerrit Rietveld, Red and Blue Chair" style="width:100%; border:4px solid #111111; margin-top:10px; background:#ffffff;" />
    <p><strong>What I observe:</strong> The chair is built from simple straight pieces and rectangular planes. The black structure and primary red, blue, and yellow colors create a strong geometric composition. The open spaces between the pieces also make the chair feel less heavy than traditional furniture.</p>
    <p><strong>How I would adapt it in a hero:</strong> I would use straight lines and rectangular blocks to create a simple grid around the main image. A limited palette inspired by the chair could add visual emphasis while keeping the headline and CTA easy to read.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="background:#f6f1ea; border:4px solid #111111; padding:18px; box-shadow:8px 8px 0 #1d4ed8;">
    <h3 style="margin-top:0; margin-bottom:12px; font-size:1.5rem;">Example 2</h3>
    <p><strong>Creator, title, date, institution:</strong> Piet Mondrian, <em>Composition with Red, Blue, Yellow, Black, and Gray</em>, 1922, original work associated with the Dutch modernist collection context.</p>
    <p><strong>Direct collection/source link:</strong> <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mondrian_-_Composition_with_Red,_Blue,_Yellow,_Black,_and_Gray,_1922.jpg">Wikimedia Commons file page</a></p>
    <p><strong>Exact image:</strong> <a href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fc/Mondrian_-_Composition_with_Red%2C_Blue%2C_Yellow%2C_Black%2C_and_Gray%2C_1922.jpg">Direct image file</a></p>
    <img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fc/Mondrian_-_Composition_with_Red%2C_Blue%2C_Yellow%2C_Black%2C_and_Gray%2C_1922.jpg" alt="Piet Mondrian, Composition with Red, Blue, Yellow, Black, and Gray" style="width:100%; border:4px solid #111111; margin-top:10px; background:#ffffff;" />
    <p><strong>What I observe:</strong> The painting uses black vertical and horizontal lines to divide the canvas into rectangular areas. Red, blue, yellow, gray, black, and white create a limited color system. The uneven arrangement of the rectangles creates balance without making the composition symmetrical.</p>
    <p><strong>How I would adapt it in a hero:</strong> I would use a simple grid with different-sized rectangular sections and a limited color palette. The main image could occupy one large section while the headline and CTA sit in separate blocks of the composition.</p>
