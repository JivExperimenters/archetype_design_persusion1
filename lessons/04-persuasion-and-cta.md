# 4. Make persuasion visible

[← Imagery](03-faces-and-imagery.md) · [Home](../README.md) · [Next: examples →](05-example-gallery.md)

**You will learn:** how to connect a reason to act with an honest next step.

## A CTA promises what happens next

A call to action asks the visitor to do something. Describe the action or result. Start with one primary action.

| Less useful | More specific | Expected destination |
|---|---|---|
| Click here | Download the walking map | The promised file |
| Get started | Choose my first route | Route selection |
| Learn more | See the workshop outline | The outline |
| Submit | Send my project inquiry | Inquiry confirmation |
| Buy now | Choose my size | Size selection before purchase |

Match the label to the next screen. Do not label an email-signup form “Download now” without explaining the signup. For a static mockup, document the intended destination.

## The principle must exist in the experience

The mechanisms below summarize [Cialdini's official overview](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/). Applications are original hypothetical examples, not claims about businesses.

| Principle | Mechanism | Example idea → CTA |
|---|---|---|
| Reciprocity | Give useful value first | Free walking map → “Get the free map” |
| Commitment / consistency | Support a voluntary choice already made | A chosen walking goal → “Plan my first walk” |
| Unity | Connect through shared identity | Campus walking group → “Find our campus routes” |
| Liking | Rapport through similarity or affinity | Hosts with shared interests → “Meet the walking hosts” |
| Authority | Relevant, verifiable expertise | Named specialist reviews a route → “See the reviewed route” |
| Social proof | Genuine behavior or experiences of others | Verified participant feedback → “Read walkers' stories” |
| Scarcity | A real limit | Actual limited-capacity walk → “Check available places” |

Only claim reviews, qualifications, participation, or limited places when supported. For fictional work, choose a mechanism you can demonstrate honestly or mark evidence as needed rather than inventing it.

A free item sets up reciprocity by giving value; downloading it does not prove a reciprocal response. A face is not social proof. “Join us” needs a meaningful shared identity to demonstrate unity.

## Same archetype, different reasons

For an Explorer walking brand:

- **Reciprocity:** “Your next small adventure starts with a free map.” → **Get the free map**.
- **Commitment:** “You chose to explore one new street this week. Plan your first walk.” → **Choose my route**. Requires a real prior choice or a clearly described flow.
- **Unity:** “For neighbors who take the long way home.” → **Find neighborhood walks**. Needs supporting community context.

The voice stays curious while the reason changes. Your assignment keeps the core offer constant; adjust framing and evidence without quietly switching products.

## Make the button readable

Use spacing and sufficient contrast. W3C specifies **4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for qualifying large text**, including text baked into images. [Contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum).

In the later website, recreate copy as HTML text and the CTA as an appropriate real link or button. A painted button is only a mockup.

## Try it in your package

Write three labels for one action. Choose the one a teammate can explain. Complete: **“After clicking, the visitor will ____.”**

**Carry forward:** put the destination in your brief and the selected label in your heroes.
