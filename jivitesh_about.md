# Jivitesh Duddu | About Page

## Core Idea

I turn original ideas into useful, playful experiences. I like to explore games, apps, UI/UX, stories, business, technology, content, and marketing by making things, learning from experiments, and sharing what works.

**In one line:** Imagine it. Make it real. Make it your own.

## Brand Archetypes

### Caregiver

The Caregiver side makes the work welcoming and useful. It focuses on helping people make progress, sharing practical resources, and making creative tools feel approachable.

### Ruler

The Ruler side brings structure and follow-through. It presents work clearly, explains decisions, and gives each project a polished, dependable frame without hiding the experiments behind it.

Together, these archetypes make the page feel generous and capable: a place to explore ideas and trust that they will be carried through.

## Visual Direction

- **Mid-Century Modernism:** Use clear geometry, purposeful layouts, and confident simplicity. Reference Paul Rand's IBM logo (1972) and Saul Bass's *Anatomy of a Murder* poster (1959).
- **Push Pin:** Add expressive illustration, energetic shapes, and hand-made character. Reference Milton Glaser's *Bob Dylan* poster (1966) and Seymour Chwast's *Dante's Inferno* (1967).
- **Swiss design and Deconstruction:** Use a disciplined grid and strong hierarchy as the foundation, then disrupt selected elements to suggest experimentation. Keep the content readable even when the composition gets playful.
- **Memphis accents:** Borrow occasional bold color and unexpected forms from Ettore Sottsass's Carlton bookcase (1981) and Michael Graves's Plaza table (1981). Treat these as playful accents rather than the page's main structure.
- **Palette:** Retro red, black, and gray, with a restrained light background and high contrast for navigation and calls to action.
- **Typography:** Archivo Black or Zico Display for expressive headings; a clean sans-serif for body copy and longer project descriptions.
- **Imagery:** Show sketches, prototypes, interface details, game-inspired graphics, and finished work. Pair process images with concise explanations so experimentation remains easy to follow.

## About Page Content

### Intro

**Headline:** I turn ideas into experiences.

**Supporting copy:** I'm Jivitesh, a creator drawn to originality and technical creativity. I build games, apps, interfaces, stories, and other experiments, sharing the process as I learn what makes an idea useful or enjoyable.

### What I Do

Introduce the kinds of work Jivitesh explores: games and apps, UI/UX, storytelling, technology, business, content, and marketing. Keep this as an invitation to explore rather than a claim of expertise in every area.

### How I Work

Show a simple project cycle: explore the idea, make a prototype, test it, and improve it. Include real examples of what changed between iterations when available.

### Selected Work

For each project, explain the challenge, Jivitesh's role, the process, and the result. Link to a working demo or published work when available, and label unfinished work as an experiment or prototype.

### Build Together

Close with a direct invitation to share an idea, explore a collaboration, or view the work. Keep the call to action specific and easy to find.

## Persuasion and Trust

- **Reciprocity:** Offer useful resources, design notes, experiments, and lessons learned so visitors receive something practical before being asked to collaborate.
- **Social proof:** Include genuine feedback, reviews, or project outcomes only when they can be verified. Attribute feedback and date metrics; omit evidence that is not available rather than implying traction.
