## Why Sage fits me

In a previous conversation, the AI suggested the **Sage** archetype because I value logical thinking above other considerations. I agreed with that choice. The Sage's focus on knowledge, understanding, and reason fits the way I want to present myself: someone who thinks carefully and can explain the reasoning behind a conclusion.

For my personal brand, I want that emphasis on logic to come across as helpful and approachable. My goal is to make ideas easier to understand while remaining willing to reconsider a conclusion when new evidence appears.

## Voice and sample phrasing

My proposed voice is thoughtful, direct, curious, and respectful. I would explain unfamiliar terms and show how I reached an answer.

- **Tagline:** “Clear thinking. Better understanding.”
- **Short introduction:** “I'm Brennan Dahmen. I value logical thinking and want to make complex ideas easier to understand.”
- **Project introduction:** “Here is the problem, the evidence I considered, and how I reached my conclusion.”
- **Invitation to collaborate:** “Let's break down the problem and work through the reasoning together.”
- **Response to feedback:** “What evidence would help us evaluate this idea?”

## Applying Cialdini's persuasion principles

A company selling a **plain white T-shirt** to a Sage audience could emphasize evidence, useful information, and thoughtful purchasing. These hypothetical examples apply [Cialdini's seven persuasion principles](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/) to that same product. Any reviews, expert endorsements, test results, or stock limits used in actual advertising would need to be genuine.

### 1. Reciprocity

**Example:** The company could offer a free guide explaining how to compare T-shirt fabric, stitching, and care requirements: “Know what to look for in a plain white T-shirt with our free buying guide.”

**Why it fits:** Giving useful information first may encourage shoppers to consider the company's shirt in return. A Sage customer could appreciate help making an informed purchase.

### 2. Commitment and consistency

**Example:** The company could invite shoppers to create a checklist of their priorities, such as durability, comfort, and price, then show how its white T-shirt meets those criteria: “Find the shirt that matches the standards you chose.”

**Why it fits:** Choosing buying criteria creates a small, voluntary commitment. Purchasing a shirt that demonstrably meets those criteria would be consistent with the Sage customer's stated priorities.

### 3. Social proof

**Example:** The company could advertise its plain white T-shirt with verified customer reviews describing fit and performance after repeated washing: “See what owners report after months of wear.”

**Why it fits:** Other buyers' experiences can reduce uncertainty about the product. Detailed reviews would give a Sage customer practical information to evaluate beyond a star rating.

### 4. Authority

**Example:** A qualified textile specialist could explain the shirt's construction and interpret documented fabric tests: “See a textile expert's analysis of our plain white T-shirt.”

**Why it fits:** Relevant expertise can lend credibility to product claims. Providing the expert's qualifications and supporting results would let a Sage customer examine the reasoning behind the recommendation.

### 5. Liking

**Example:** An approachable company designer could explain why they care about everyday details: “I wanted to understand what makes a white T-shirt comfortable, so I started with the fabric. Let's take a closer look.”

**Why it fits:** A friendly person who shares the customer's curiosity can make the brand more likable. The shared interest in understanding how things work could appeal to a Sage audience.

### 6. Scarcity

**Example:** If stock were genuinely limited, the company could advertise: “Only 20 plain white T-shirts remain in this production run. The next batch arrives in six weeks,” alongside sizing and fabric details.

**Why it fits:** Limited availability can encourage a timely decision. Clear stock and restock information would allow a Sage customer to weigh buying now against waiting.

### 7. Unity

**Example:** The company could build a customer community that shares clothing-care findings and helps evaluate its plain white T-shirt: “For fellow curious consumers: help us understand what makes an everyday shirt worth buying.”

**Why it fits:** A shared identity as curious, thoughtful consumers can create belonging. Inviting customers to contribute observations would connect the shirt with a community that values learning together.
