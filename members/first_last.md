# Personal Archetypes

## Minh Nguyen - Magician

**Core idea**

Innovation as a way to create spectacle and amusement for others. Asking why I want an archetype, how others might see me, and what I am creating for helped clarify that I want my work to surprise, delight, and make an impression.

**Visual direction**

- Pair legible serif and sans-serif typefaces.
- Use neutral colors that still feel lively, with a warm, distinctive accent.
- Keep the voice grounded and accepting: “It is what it is.”

**How to build trust**

Give away useful tools and ideas as an act of reciprocity. Consistently deliver value, then let social proof demonstrate the quality and reliability of that work.

## Brennan Dahmen - Sage

**Core idea**

I value logical thinking above other considerations, and the Sage feels like a fitting archetype. Its focus on knowledge, understanding, and reason reflects how I want to present myself: someone who thinks carefully and can explain the reasoning behind a conclusion.

**Visual direction**

- Use books and diagrams as visual motifs.
- Build the palette around navy and ivory, with teal accents.
- Set headings in Georgia and body text in Arial.
- Sample phrasing: “Clear thinking. Better understanding.”

**How to build trust**

Share useful guides to encourage reciprocity. Show evidence and reasoning to establish authority, and include genuine peer feedback as social proof that the explanations help others.

## Jivitesh Duddu - Creator

**Core idea**

The Creator fits my interest in originality, experimentation, technical creativity, and turning ideas into real experiences. I enjoy making games, apps, UI/UX, stories, and other projects(business, tech,content, marketing) in my own way.

**Visual direction**

- Aim for an experimental, game-inspired style that feels distinctive and appealing.
- Combine sketch-like details and abstract forms into an engaging visual language.
- Use a retro-gaming palette with red and comic-inspired black and gray.
- Consider Archivo Black or Zico Display for expressive display type, paired with a modern, clean sans-serif for readability.

**How to build trust**

Showcase projects alongside feedback, ratings, reviews, and views to establish credibility with social proof. Give back to the community with useful design insights, resources, and creative ideas with reciprocity.

## Omari Sellers - Creator

**Core idea**

The Creator fits my interest in creating things and turning ideas into something real. I am interested in videography and content creation, business, technology, entrepreneurship, and eventually starting my own businesses. I also enjoy experimenting with different ideas and finding my own way to approach problems. I want to build rather than simply follow what already exists or work under someone else.

**Visual direction**

- Use cameras and video-production equipment, cars and roads, laptops and technology, sketches, blank canvases, and unfinished designs as imagery.
- Show behind-the-scenes content to represent the process of creating something.
- Use black and white with a bright accent color such as blue or orange for a clean, modern appearance with standout elements.
- Use a clean, modern sans-serif font such as Montserrat, Poppins, or Inter.
- Sample phrasing: “Build it. Learn from it. Keep creating.”

**How to build trust**

Provide useful information for free, such as tips about content creation, technology, or starting projects. Publicly set goals and document progress so the brand feels authentic and demonstrates follow-through. Giving people something useful first can encourage them to engage with the brand and eventually support the work.
